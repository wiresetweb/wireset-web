The 7 things every local service homepage needs above the fold

What a customer has to see in the first three seconds — before they scroll, and before they bounce to your competitor.

Websites June 11, 2026 · 6 min read · By Wireset Web

"Above the fold" is the part of your homepage a visitor sees before scrolling. On a phone, that's a small window — and it's where the decision to stay or leave gets made in about three seconds. Here's what has to be in it.

The seven non-negotiables

  • 1. Who you are and what you do. In plain words, instantly. "Northgate Heating & Air — HVAC repair & install." Not a clever slogan that makes them guess.
  • 2. Where you work. Your city or service area, visible immediately. Local customers need to know they're in the right place before anything else.
  • 3. A way to contact you, right now. A tap-to-call button or a quote button — not buried in a menu, not three scrolls down.
  • 4. One reason to trust you. Years in business, a license number, "family-owned," a star rating. One credibility signal up top does a lot of work.
  • 5. A clear next step. One obvious action you want them to take, styled so it's impossible to miss.
  • 6. It loads fast. The best above-the-fold content is worthless if it shows up after a four-second wait.
  • 7. It works on a phone. Most local searches happen on mobile. If it's only right on a desktop, it's wrong where it counts.

What does not belong up there

Just as important is what you leave out. The top of your homepage is the most valuable space you own — don't waste it on a giant slideshow nobody waits for, a stock photo of a handshake, a wall of text, or a cookie banner that covers the whole screen. Every element up top should earn its place by helping the customer decide to call you.

You get three seconds and one small screen. Spend them on the customer, not on decoration.
53% of mobile visitors leave a page that takes more than three seconds to load — before they ever see your offer. (Google)

The short version

  • Say who you are, what you do, and where — instantly and in plain words
  • Put a call or quote button up top, not in a menu
  • Show one trust signal: rating, license, years, or “family-owned”
  • Make the next step obvious and impossible to miss
  • It has to be fast and right on a phone, or none of it matters
