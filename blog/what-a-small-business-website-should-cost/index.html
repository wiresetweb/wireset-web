What a small business website should actually cost in 2026

From free builders to $10k agencies — where the real value sits, and how to avoid both overpaying and buying something that never gets you hired.

Pricing June 11, 2026 · 6 min read · By Wireset Web

Ask five people what a website costs and you'll get five answers between "free" and "ten grand." Both extremes are real, and both can be the wrong call. Here's an honest map of what you're actually paying for at each price.

The price tiers, and what each really buys

  • Free DIY builders. Tempting, and fine for a placeholder. The hidden cost is your time and the ceiling: slow templates, generic design, and a site that looks like ten thousand others. You're the web designer now, on nights and weekends.
  • $500–$1,500, done for you. The sweet spot for most local service businesses: a fast, custom-built, mobile-ready site that you own, without paying for overhead you'll never use. This is where value peaks.
  • $5,000–$10,000+ agencies. Real craft, but you're also funding account managers, sales teams, and offices. Worth it for complex builds; overkill for a plumber who needs to get found and get called.
1 job is what a good site costs most service businesses. One new customer it brings in usually pays for the whole build.

What you should never pay for

Some line items are pure margin for the seller and pure cost for you:

  • Rented access to your own site. Paying a monthly just to keep a site you can never take with you. (We wrote a whole piece on this trap.)
  • Guaranteed SEO rankings. Nobody can promise a #1 spot. Anyone who does is selling you something they don't control.
  • Features you'll never touch. A booking engine, a blog, a members area — great if you'll use them, dead weight if you won't.
The right price isn’t the lowest or the highest. It’s the one that gets you hired and lets you walk away.

The real cost question

Don't ask "what does a website cost?" Ask "what does not having a good one cost?" One slow week, one customer who clicked your competitor because your site took five seconds to load, one job lost to a business that simply looked more credible online. Measured that way, a well-built site is one of the cheapest things a local business buys.

The short version

  • Most local service businesses are best served in the $500–$1,500 range
  • Free builders cost you in time, speed, and a generic look
  • Agencies are worth it for complex builds, overkill for getting found and called
  • Never pay for rented access, ranking guarantees, or features you won’t use
  • The real cost to weigh is the jobs a bad site loses you
